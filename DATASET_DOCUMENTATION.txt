AIRFLOW AI — AQI PREDICTION MODEL TRAINING DATASETS DOCUMENTATION
================================================================================

This document outlines the official datasets and sources used to train the 
AirFlow AI machine learning models, calculate pollutant sub-indices, and power 
real-time hourly forecasts.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
PART 1: REAL-TIME APIS USED IN THE WEB APPLICATION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

1. Open-Meteo Air Quality API
   - Purpose: Fetches live concentrations of key air pollutants (PM2.5, PM10, 
     NO2, SO2, CO, O3, Dust) for any searched city or GPS coordinates.
   - Endpoint: https://air-quality-api.open-meteo.com/v1/air-quality

2. Open-Meteo Weather Forecast API
   - Purpose: Fetches real-time weather drivers including temperature, 
     humidity, wind speed, wind direction, and surface pressure.
   - Endpoint: https://api.open-meteo.com/v1/forecast

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
PART 2: MODEL TRAINING DATASETS (POST-2022 ALL-INDIA)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The machine learning models (XGBoost Classifier, Regressor, and Ridge baseline) 
are trained using post-2022 data covering monitoring stations across India:

1. IMD & CPCB Air Quality Daily Dataset (Post-2022)
   - File: `datasets/latest_aqi_daily_2020_2026.csv`
   - Description: Daily aggregated air quality and weather measurements across 
     Indian cities from official CAAQMS stations and IMD records.
   - Features Used: PM2.5, PM10, NO2, SO2, CO, O3, Temperature, Humidity, 
     Wind Speed, Month.

2. IMD & CPCB Air Quality Hourly Dataset (Post-2022)
   - File: `datasets/latest_aqi_hourly_2020_2026.csv`
   - Description: High-resolution continuous hourly observations capturing 
     morning traffic peaks, midday dispersion, and evening stagnation.
   - Features Used: Hourly pollutant levels, diurnal atmospheric cycles, hour of day.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
PART 3: SUMMARY OF FEATURES USED IN MODEL TRAINING (20 TOTAL)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

1. Base Pollutant Concentrations (6):
   - PM2.5, PM10, NO2, SO2, CO, O3

2. Derived Sub-Indices & Domain Ratios (9):
   - PM2.5 Sub-Index (Individual CPCB air quality score for PM2.5)
   - PM10 Sub-Index (Individual CPCB air quality score for PM10)
   - NO2 Sub-Index (Individual CPCB air quality score for NO2)
   - SO2 Sub-Index (Individual CPCB air quality score for SO2)
   - CO Sub-Index (Individual CPCB air quality score for CO)
   - O3 Sub-Index (Individual CPCB air quality score for Ozone)
   - Maximum Sub-Index (Highest sub-index value, determining overall AQI)
   - PM Ratio (PM2.5 / PM10 ratio, indicating combustion vs dust)
   - Oxidant Sum (NO2 + O3 combined photochemical intensity)

3. Meteorological & Temporal Drivers (5):
   - Temperature (°C), Humidity (%), Wind Speed (km/h)
   - Month of year, Hour of day

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
HOW TO RETRAIN THE MODEL:
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
To retrain the model and export updated weights:
1. Ensure Python with pandas, scikit-learn, xgboost, and joblib is installed.
2. Run the unified training script:
   `python train_model.py`
3. This automatically updates `ml_model.json` (for browser Web Worker) and `ml_model.pkl`.
