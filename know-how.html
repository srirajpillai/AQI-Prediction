What is SHAP?

SHAP (SHapley Additive exPlanations) is a method that explains individual predictions by quantifying each feature's contribution. Positive values increase the predicted AQI, while negative values decrease it.

How to Read

The longer the bar, the stronger the influence. Yellow bars push the AQI higher (worse air quality), while blue bars push the AQI lower (better air quality). The factors are sorted by absolute impact.

Why It Matters

Understanding which factors drive air quality helps policymakers target specific pollution sources. For example, if PM2.5 dominates, reducing vehicle emissions could be prioritized.

Pollutant Health Encyclopedia

In-depth medical information about each pollutant's impact

PM2.5 — Fine Particulate Matter

Sources: Vehicle exhaust, industrial emissions, biomass burning, cooking fumes
Size: <2.5μm — 30x thinner than human hair. Enters bloodstream.
Health Effects
  • Short-term: Eye irritation, cough, throat soreness, chest tightness
  • Long-term: Decreased lung function, chronic bronchitis, heart attacks
  • Severe: Lung cancer, premature death, cognitive decline, dementia
WHO: Annual ≤5 μg/m³, 24hr ≤15 μg/m³

PM10 — Coarse Particulate

Sources: Road dust, agricultural activity, mining, pollen, mold
Size: <10μm — enters nose and throat
Health Effects
  • Upper Respiratory: Nasal congestion, sinusitis, aggravated rhinitis
  • Lower Respiratory: Aggravates asthma, bronchitis, COPD
  • Occupational: Silicosis in miners, pneumoconiosis in construction
WHO: Annual ≤15 μg/m³, 24hr ≤45 μg/m³

O3 — Ground-Level Ozone

Sources: NOx + VOCs in sunlight (secondary pollutant)
Health Effects
  • Respiratory: Airway inflammation, reduced lung function
  • Chronic: Permanent lung damage, asthma development in children
  • Athletes: Higher exposure during exercise due to increased breathing
WHO: 8hr mean ≤60 μg/m³

NO2 — Nitrogen Dioxide

Sources: Diesel engines, power plants, gas stoves
Health Effects
  • Airways: Inflames lung lining, reduces immunity to infections
  • Children: Increased asthma risk, respiratory symptoms in healthy children
  • Indoor: Gas stoves produce dangerous NO₂ levels in unventilated kitchens
WHO: Annual ≤10 μg/m³, 24hr ≤25 μg/m³

SO2 — Sulfur Dioxide

Sources: Coal/oil power plants, metal smelting, volcanic eruptions
Health Effects
  • Immediate: Wheezing, chest tightness within minutes
  • Asthma: Very potent trigger — severe bronchospasm at low levels
  • Secondary: Converts to sulfate PM2.5, causes acid rain
WHO: 24hr ≤40 μg/m³

CO — Carbon Monoxide

Sources: Incomplete combustion — vehicles, generators, gas heaters, fires
Health Effects
  • Mechanism: Binds hemoglobin 200x stronger than O₂ — starves organs
  • Mild: Headaches, dizziness, confusion, fatigue
  • Severe: Loss of consciousness, brain damage, death
  • Heart: Triggers angina and arrhythmia in coronary patients
WHO: 24hr ≤4 mg/m³
