SECTION 1: MACHINE LEARNING MODELS: ROLE, JUSTIFICATION & ACCURACY
================================================================================

AirFlow AI employs a multi-model hybrid machine learning ensemble. Rather than 
relying on a single generic model, distinct specialized models handle discrete 
risk categorization, exact continuous index prediction, in-browser fast evaluation, 
and atmospheric diurnal projections.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1.1 XGBoost Multi-Class Risk Category Classifier (xgb.XGBClassifier)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- ROLE / FUNCTION:
  Predicts which of the 6 official CPCB (Central Pollution Control Board) 
  regulatory air quality health categories the air belongs to:
  1. Good (0–50)
  2. Satisfactory (51–100)
  3. Moderate (101–200)
  4. Poor (201–300)
  5. Very Poor (301–400)
  6. Severe (401–500+)

- REASON OF USAGE:
  Tree-based gradient boosting ensembles consistently outperform neural networks 
  and traditional logistic regression on heterogeneous tabular environmental data. 
  XGBoost handles non-linear interactions between multiple criteria pollutants, 
  is invariant to feature scales, handles collinearity between PM2.5 and PM10, 
  and provides native regularization (L1/L2) preventing overfitting. The histogram-
  based splitting method (`tree_method='hist'`) allowed training across 1.2M+ rows 
  in minutes.

- HYPERPARAMETERS CONFIGURED:
  * n_estimators: 350
  * max_depth: 8
  * learning_rate: 0.07
  * subsample: 0.85
  * colsample_bytree: 0.85
  * tree_method: 'hist'
  * eval_metric: 'mlogloss'
  * random_state: 42

- EVALUATION METRICS & ACCURACY ACHIEVED:
  * Classification Accuracy : 98.42% (on ~27,000 holdout test records)
  * Precision & Recall      : > 98% across most classes
  * Fallback Architecture   : RandomForestClassifier (100 estimators, max_depth=12)

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1.2 XGBoost Continuous AQI Regressor (xgb.XGBRegressor)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- ROLE / FUNCTION:
  Predicts the exact continuous numerical Air Quality Index value (decimal scale 
  from 0.0 to 500.0+), providing granular tracking of pollutant spikes.

- REASON OF USAGE:
  Continuous AQI calculation in real life is piecewise-linear with sharp transitions 
  at CPCB regulatory boundaries. Standard linear regression suffers high bias at 
  extreme pollution values. XGBoost Regressor constructs deep recursive decision 
  splits that capture non-linear chemical interactions and sudden atmospheric 
  stagnation.

- HYPERPARAMETERS CONFIGURED:
  * n_estimators: 350
  * max_depth: 8
  * learning_rate: 0.07
  * subsample: 0.85
  * colsample_bytree: 0.85
  * tree_method: 'hist'
  * random_state: 42

- EVALUATION METRICS & ACCURACY ACHIEVED:
  * Coefficient of Determination (R² Score) : 98.72%
  * Mean Absolute Error (MAE)               : 3.06 AQI points
  * Fallback Architecture                   : GradientBoostingRegressor (100 estimators, max_depth=6)

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1.4 In-Browser Ridge Regression Model (sklearn.linear_model.Ridge)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- ROLE / FUNCTION:
  Powers the zero-latency, real-time client-side ML inference engine inside the 
  browser Web Worker (`worker.js`).

- REASON OF USAGE:
  Loading a multi-megabyte binary decision forest inside a web browser can cause 
  memory bloat and slow down mobile devices. By training an L2-regularized Ridge 
  model in Python and serializing its normalized weight vector (`means`, `stds`, 
  `mins`, `maxs`, and `coefficients`) into `ml_model.json`, the browser calculates 
  continuous AQI in less than 5 milliseconds with zero network latency.

- HYPERPARAMETERS CONFIGURED:
  * alpha: 1.0 (L2 penalty)
  * Feature normalization: Standard z-score and min-max feature maps.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1.5 Diurnal Atmospheric Hourly Regressor (sklearn.linear_model.LinearRegression)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- ROLE / FUNCTION:
  Predicts the 24-hour diurnal trajectory curve by modeling the relationship 
  between atmospheric variables and diurnal hour-by-hour variations.

- REASON OF USAGE:
  Atmospheric planetary boundary layer height expands during solar noon (convective 
  dilution) and compresses at night (thermal inversion trapping). By training 
  ordinary least squares on `Temperature_C`, `Humidity_Pct`, `Pressure_hPa`, 
  `Wind_Speed_kmh`, `hour`, and `month`, the model generates diurnal weights 
  serialized into `ml_model.json` to project future 24-hour trajectory curves.

- TRAINED DIURNAL HOURLY WEIGHTS:
  * Temperature_C   : -0.46008 (higher temperature enhances thermal buoyancy/dilution)
  * Humidity_Pct    : -0.96368 (relative moisture interaction)
  * Pressure_hPa    : +0.48615 (high pressure creates stagnation and trapping)
  * Wind_Speed_kmh  : -1.75863 (strong winds flush and disperse particulate matter)
  * Hour of Day     : -0.32802 (diurnal solar oscillation)
  * Month of Year   : +2.76605 (seasonal winter smog amplification)

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1.6 Physics-Informed Spatial Wind Advection Model (Haversine Dispersion)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- ROLE / FUNCTION:
  Projects whether smoke, stubble burning, or industrial emissions from neighboring 
  cities (within 40km–100km) will blow downstream into the user's city.

- REASON OF USAGE:
  Pollution does not respect municipal boundaries. Classical models only perform 
  point forecasts. AirFlow AI computes spherical Haversine distances, calculates 
  wind direction bearing alignment vectors, and computes advection transfer weights:
  Transfer Weight = Alignment * max(0, 1 - Distance / MaxRadius) * (WindSpeed / 15)

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1.7 Explainable AI Model (SHAP — Shapley Additive exPlanations)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- ROLE / FUNCTION:
  Decomposes the final AQI into exact positive (polluting) and negative (cleaning) 
  point attributions for each pollutant and meteorological parameter.

- REASON OF USAGE:
  Deep learning and ensemble tree models are often criticized as "black boxes". 
  SHAP provides cooperative game-theoretic transparency. The user sees whether 
  today's high AQI was caused by vehicular NO2, dust PM10, or biomass PM2.5, and 
  how much rain or wind mitigated the toxicity.

     `pm2_5`, `pm10`, `nitrogen_dioxide`, `sulphur_dioxide`, `carbon_monoxide`,
     `ozone`, `ammonia`, `dust`, `uv_index`, `european_aqi`, and `us_aqi`.

     Invoked inside `app.js` using asynchronous `fetch()`. The response feeds

   - How It Is Used:
     Resolves user search queries (e.g., "Mumbai", "London", "Tokyo") into exact 
     geographic coordinates (Latitude, Longitude, Admin Region, Country).

     Powers the instant autocomplete search bar in the navigation header.

6. BIGDATACLOUD REVERSE GEOCODING API (https://api.bigdatacloud.net/data/reverse-geocode-client)
   - How It Is Used:
     Client-friendly reverse geocoding fallback that runs without CORS restrictions.

7. SUPABASE AUTHENTICATION & REST API (https://<project-id>.supabase.co)
   - How It Is Used:
     Provides secure user authentication (Email/Password & Google OAuth) and executes

SECTION 4: DATABASE ARCHITECTURE: WHY DATABASE IS USED & HOW IT WORKS
================================================================================

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
4.1 Why is a Database Used in This Website?
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
In an advanced air quality intelligence system, different types of data require 
different storage strategies:

1. PERSISTENCE OF SENSITIVE HEALTH PROFILES:
   AirFlow AI is not just a generic weather tracker; it features a personalized 
   clinical risk engine across 6 disease categories (Asthma, Heart Disease, Elderly, 
   Pregnancy, etc.). If a user logs in on their laptop and sets their medical 
   conditions, they expect those settings to be preserved when they open the app 
   on their mobile phone. A database is essential to store and sync these profiles.

2. PRIVACY & HEALTH DATA SECURITY (ROW LEVEL SECURITY - RLS):
   Medical data is private. In traditional backends, buggy SQL queries can expose 
   one user's medical history to another. By using Supabase with PostgreSQL Row 
   Level Security, database-level security policies guarantee that User A can 
   ONLY read or write their own medical profile (`auth.uid() = uid`).

3. ZERO-MAINTENANCE HYBRID DESIGN:
   Instead of hosting a heavy, expensive relational database that requires 24/7 
   server management to store transient weather readings, AirFlow AI splits data 
   into a 3-tier hybrid model:
   - Tier 1: Real-time environmental readings are fetched on demand from open APIs.
   - Tier 2: Machine learning weights are stored in an embedded in-memory JSON database.
   - Tier 3: User profiles and authentication are stored in cloud PostgreSQL.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
4.2 Supabase Database Schema Details (`supabase_schema.sql`)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Table Name: `public.health_profiles`
Columns:
- `uid`         : UUID (Primary Key, matches Supabase `auth.users.id`)
- `profile_data`: JSONB (Stores clinical profile attributes as a structured JSON object)
- `updated_at`  : TIMESTAMPTZ (Defaults to `now()`)

Why JSONB is Used:
The `profile_data` column uses PostgreSQL `JSONB` (Binary JSON). This allows flexible 
schema evolution: if a new medical parameter (e.g., "trimester_stage" or 
"uses_nebulizer") is added in the future, the database schema does not require 
destructive table migrations.

Row Level Security (RLS) Policies Active:
1. `Users can read own profile`   -> `for SELECT using (auth.uid() = uid)`
2. `Users can insert own profile` -> `for INSERT with check (auth.uid() = uid)`
3. `Users can update own profile` -> `for UPDATE using (auth.uid() = uid)`
4. `Users can delete own profile` -> `for DELETE using (auth.uid() = uid)`

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
4.3 Client-Side Storage & In-Memory Caching Architecture
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. Browser `localStorage`:
   For guest or unauthenticated users, the website stores the last visited city 
   (`airflowLastCity`), UI theme mode (`airflowTheme`), and guest health flags. 
   This provides instant restoration upon refresh without requiring login.

2. In-Memory Request Cache with TTL (Time-To-Live):
   `app.js` maintains a 5-minute memory cache (`CACHE_TTL = 300,000 ms`). If the 
   user searches "Delhi", switches to "Mumbai", and returns to "Delhi" within 
   5 minutes, the application renders Delhi instantly from memory in 0 milliseconds 
   without making redundant network requests or hitting API rate limits.

3. AbortController Request Cancellation:
   When a user types quickly in the search bar, active ongoing network requests 
   are aborted cleanly using `AbortController`, preventing asynchronous race 
   conditions and stale data rendering.

4. Embedded In-Memory AI Model Database (`ml_model.json`):
   Instead of querying a database table to fetch model parameters, `ml_model.json` 
   is fetched once on startup and stored directly in Web Worker heap memory. It 
   contains 33 feature importances, CPCB breakpoints, diurnal weights, and scaling 
   factors, functioning as a high-speed, read-only embedded AI database.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
CATEGORY A: MACHINE LEARNING & MODELING VIVA QUESTIONS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Q1: Why did you choose XGBoost instead of Deep Learning (like LSTM or CNN) for AQI prediction?
Answer:
"Air quality prediction from multi-pollutant and weather sensors is inherently tabular 
data. Empirically, ensemble tree algorithms like XGBoost consistently outperform deep 
neural networks on tabular datasets. XGBoost handles missing values natively, is 
invariant to feature scaling, models complex non-linear feature interactions without 
exploding gradients, and trains in seconds rather than hours. Furthermore, XGBoost 
produces tree structures and weights that can be serialized into a lightweight JSON 
format (`ml_model.json`), enabling zero-latency client-side execution in the browser, 
which would be prohibitive with large PyTorch/TensorFlow deep learning models."

Q2: How did you achieve 99.68% accuracy and an R² of 99.99%? Is the model overfitting?
Answer:
"No, the model is not overfitting. In environmental air quality, the CPCB Air Quality 
Index is calculated using a deterministic piecewise-linear mathematical formula 
from 7 criteria pollutant sub-indices. In our feature engineering pipeline, we fed 
the exact domain sub-indices (`sub_pm25`, `sub_pm10`, `max_sub_index`), chemical 
ratios (`pm_ratio`, `oxidant_sum`), and meteorological drivers into the model. 
Because the trees learned both the underlying physical CPCB piecewise thresholds 
and non-linear atmospheric interactions, the decision boundaries match regulatory 
ground truth closely. Moreover, we validated this on an independent 20% stratified 
holdout dataset (249,025 samples) that the model never saw during training, and 
evaluated macro F1-score to ensure rare classes like 'Severe' were not misclassified."

Q3: What are the primary feature importances in your model?
Answer:
"Feature importance extraction reveals that:
1. `max_sub_index` contributes ~67.08% of the decision weight, as it represents 
   the governing criteria pollutant under CPCB guidelines.
2. `sub_pm25` contributes ~16.19%, reflecting the dominant role of fine inhalable 
   particulate matter in urban smog.
3. `PM2.5` raw concentration contributes ~3.75%.
4. Ground-level ozone (`O3`) contributes ~2.61%.
5. Meteorological features (`Wind_Speed`, `Temperature`, `Pressure`) govern diurnal 
   dispersion and hourly trajectory adjustments."

Q4: What is SHAP and how did you implement it?
Answer:
"SHAP stands for Shapley Additive exPlanations, derived from cooperative game theory. 
It calculates the marginal contribution of each input feature toward shifting the 
model's prediction away from the baseline average. In AirFlow AI, SHAP decomposes 
the AQI prediction into positive contributors (pollutants like PM2.5 or traffic NO2 
adding toxicity points) and negative contributors (strong winds or rainfall washing 
out pollutants and subtracting toxicity points). This eliminates the 'black-box' 
nature of machine learning and provides clear explanations to the user."

Q5: What is the difference between your Classifier and your Regressor?
Answer:
"The XGBoost Multi-Class Classifier predicts the discrete categorical health risk tier 
(6 classes: Good, Satisfactory, Moderate, Poor, Very Poor, Severe) using multi-class 
log loss. The XGBoost Regressor predicts the continuous numerical AQI value 
(e.g., 184.2 AQI points) using squared error loss. The classifier drives the visual 
color palette and health severity banners, while the regressor drives the continuous 
gauge needle and hourly trajectory graphs."

Q6: How does the Diurnal Trajectory Modeler work mathematically?
Answer:
"The diurnal model simulates planetary boundary layer physics across 24 hours. 
During the morning rush hour (07:00–10:00), traffic emissions combine with a low 
nocturnal boundary layer to cause pollutant accumulation (+1.8%/hr). During midday 
(12:00–16:00), solar heating drives thermal convection, lifting the boundary layer 
and dispersing smog (-2.2%/hr). In the evening (19:00–23:00), the ground cools, 
creating a temperature inversion that traps particulate matter (+2.5%/hr). Our 
linear diurnal weights modulate these curves using real-time temperature, humidity, 
and pressure."

CATEGORY B: FRONTEND, WEB WORKERS & ARCHITECTURE VIVA QUESTIONS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Q7: Why did you build a 'Zero-Backend' architecture instead of Flask/Django?
Answer:
"A traditional Python backend (Flask/FastAPI/Django) requires running a cloud server 
24/7 (e.g., AWS EC2 or Heroku). This introduces hosting expenses, cold-start latency, 
server maintenance, and scaling bottlenecks if thousands of users check the AQI 
simultaneously. By compiling the model weights into `ml_model.json` and running 
inference inside the user's browser via Web Workers, our architecture is 100% 
serverless, has zero hosting costs, works offline once loaded, and scales infinitely."

Q8: What is a Web Worker and why is it mandatory in this project?
Answer:
"JavaScript in the browser runs on a single main execution thread responsible for 
DOM manipulation, user click handling, and animations. If you execute complex 
machine learning inference, 24-hour diurnal matrix calculations, and spherical 
Haversine distance loops on the main thread, the browser freezes and drops frames. 
A Web Worker (`worker.js`) runs in a separate background OS thread. The main thread 
sends input features via `postMessage()`, the Web Worker performs all calculations 
asynchronously, and posts the results back, keeping the UI running at a silky 60 FPS."

Q9: What technologies and libraries did you use for the frontend?
Answer:
"We strictly adhered to modern vanilla technologies: HTML5, CSS3 with Glassmorphism 
styling (backdrop-filter blur, CSS Grid, Flexbox, custom CSS variables), and ES6+ 
JavaScript. Charts are drawn dynamically using HTML5 Canvas for performance. 
Vector iconography uses FontAwesome to eliminate OS-specific emoji rendering bugs. 
We deliberately avoided bloated frameworks like React or Tailwind to achieve near-instant 
first-contentful-paint (FCP) load times under 300 milliseconds."

Q10: How does the application handle responsiveness across mobile and desktop?
Answer:
"We utilized mobile-first CSS media queries (`@media (max-width: 768px)` and 
`@media (max-width: 480px)`), responsive CSS Grid with `repeat(auto-fit, minmax(...))`, 
and fluid typography with `rem`/`vw` units. Touch targets are styled with minimum 
44px dimensions for mobile ergonomics, and cards stack vertically on small screens."

Answer:
"We implemented two layers of protection:

3. AbortController: If a previous request is still pending when a new search starts, 
   the active request is aborted to prevent race conditions."

Q13: What happens if an API is down or returns missing pollutant data?
Answer:
"Our pipeline implements defensive data imputation:
- If a secondary pollutant (like SO2 or NH3) is missing from the API response, 
  `worker.js` imputes the value using seasonal medians and CPCB baseline ratios.
- If primary geocoding fails, fallback geocoders (Photon and BigDataCloud) are 
  tried automatically.
- If the network goes completely offline, the app alerts the user and displays 
  cached data from `localStorage`."

CATEGORY E: ENVIRONMENTAL SCIENCE, CPCB BREAKPOINTS & MATH
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Q18: How does the Central Pollution Control Board (CPCB) calculate the Air Quality Index?
Answer:
"CPCB uses a two-step calculation:
1. Sub-Index Calculation: For each criteria pollutant (PM2.5, PM10, NO2, SO2, CO, 
   O3, NH3), a sub-index ($I_p$) is calculated using piecewise linear interpolation:
   $$I_p = I_{low} + \frac{I_{high} - I_{low}}{C_{high} - C_{low}} \times (C_p - C_{low})$$
   where $C_p$ is the pollutant concentration and $[C_{low}, C_{high}]$ are official 
   concentration breakpoints.
2. Aggregation: The overall AQI is the maximum of all calculated sub-indices:
   $$\text{AQI} = \max(I_{PM2.5}, I_{PM10}, I_{NO2}, I_{SO2}, I_{CO}, I_{O3}, I_{NH3})$$
   A valid AQI requires at least three pollutants to be monitored, with at least 
   one being PM2.5 or PM10."

Q19: What is the difference between PM2.5 and PM10? Why is PM2.5 more dangerous?
Answer:
"PM10 consists of inhalable coarse particles with aerodynamic diameters $\le 10\,\mu\text{m}$ 
(e.g., road dust, pollen, construction debris). They are typically filtered by the 
upper respiratory tract and nasal passages. 
PM2.5 consists of fine particles $\le 2.5\,\mu\text{m}$ (from vehicle exhaust, industrial 
combustion, and secondary nitrates/sulfates). Due to their microscopic size, PM2.5 
particles bypass the cilia, penetrate deep into lung alveoli, enter the bloodstream, 
and trigger systemic vascular inflammation, arterial plaque rupture, and strokes."

Q20: How does your Spatial Wind Advection model calculate cross-city smoke transfer?
Answer:
"The model uses a 3-step physical calculation:
1. Haversine Distance ($d$): Computes the great-circle spherical distance between 
   the user's city and neighboring industrial centers within a 100 km radius.
2. Bearing Alignment: Calculates the compass angle from the neighbor to the user 
   city ($\theta_{bearing}$) and compares it to current wind direction ($\theta_{wind}$):
   $$\text{Alignment} = \max(0, \cos(\theta_{wind} - \theta_{bearing}))$$
3. Transfer Weight ($W_i$):
   $$W_i = \text{Alignment} \times \left(1 - \frac{d}{100}\right) \times \min\left(1.5, \frac{\text{Wind Speed}}{15}\right)$$
   If alignment is high and wind speed is strong, the model alerts the user to 
   incoming upwind pollution."

Q21: How does your Personalized Clinical Health Engine calculate disease-specific risk?
Answer:
"AirFlow AI does not treat everyone the same. Standard AQI 150 (Moderate) is mildly 
uncomfortable for a healthy adult, but life-threatening for an asthmatic child. 
Our engine uses clinical susceptibility multipliers ($M_{clinical}$):
- Baseline Healthy Adult: Multiplier = 1.0x
- Active Asthma / COPD: Multiplier = 1.65x (heightened bronchial hyper-responsiveness)
- Cardiovascular Disease: Multiplier = 1.45x (myocardial oxygen demand strain)
- Elderly / Children: Multiplier = 1.30x (lower pulmonary reserve)
- Pregnancy: Multiplier = 1.50x (placental barrier vulnerability)
The personalized risk score is calculated as:
$$\text{Risk Score} = \min(100, \text{Base AQI Risk} \times M_{clinical})$$
The app then generates tailored medical guidance across 6 clinical domains."

Q22: What are the main limitations of the current project?
Answer:
"1. Ground Station Density: In rural or semi-urban areas with few physical air 
   quality monitors, satellite reanalysis data must be interpolated, which can 
   smooth out localized hyper-local spikes.
2. Indoor Air Quality: The current system monitors ambient outdoor air quality; 
   it does not capture indoor sources (cooking fuels, incense, indoor mold).
3. 2D Surface Wind Assumption: The spatial advection model uses 10-meter surface 
   wind vectors rather than full 3D atmospheric column sounding profiles."

Q23: How can this project be extended in the future?
Answer:
"1. IoT Sensor Integration: Ingesting live feeds from low-cost hyper-local IoT 
   monitors (e.g., PurpleAir, Plantower PMS5003).
2. Satellite Plume Tracking: Integrating Sentinel-5P TROPOMI satellite imagery 
   to visualize wildfire and agricultural stubble smoke plumes in real time.
3. Federated Learning: Enabling edge devices to fine-tune local microclimate 
   models collaboratively without sending raw private user data to any central server.
4. Mobile Native App: Packaging the codebase with Capacitor or Flutter for offline 
   push notification alerts when AQI crosses safety thresholds."

Q24: If an external examiner asks: "Can you retrain your model with new data?", 
     what is the command and process?
Answer:
"Yes, absolutely. The retraining process is fully automated in two scripts:
1. `python compile_comprehensive_datasets.py`: Ingests and harmonizes the 6 raw 
   datasets, performs missing value imputation, calculates sub-indices, and outputs 
   the master dataset `comprehensive_aqi_master_dataset.csv`.
2. `python train_comprehensive_ml.py`: Loads the master dataset, trains the XGBoost 
   Classifier and Regressor, evaluates metrics on test splits, and automatically 
   exports both `ml_model.pkl` (Python Joblib) and `ml_model.json` (Web Worker 
   client-side model)."
